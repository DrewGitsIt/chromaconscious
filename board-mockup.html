this is the right move

It answers the question you raised — the labels now teach the vocabulary instead of assuming it — but the bigger payoff is that it collapses three concepts into one:

colors with rolesslots you filled
invented colorsslots the smith derived
unused colorsyour bench

"Invented" stops being a concept needing its own lane, its own explanation and its own verb. It's just a slot the smith filled. And "unused" stops being a dead end on a color you cared enough to add — it's a bench, which answers "why isn't my color doing anything?" at a glance.

tryRiff. Only the dashed slots re-roll; yours sit perfectly still. That's the clearest statement of what riff does that any of these rounds has produced — better than the cross-fade I was pleased with two sheets ago.

what dropping order killed

Real deletions, not just visual ones:

  • reorderCandidate and remapAfterMove in App.tsx
  • drag-to-reorder + the grip in CandidateStrip
  • the "higher rows claim roles first" model the user had to infer

And drag gets stronger by losing a job: it used to mean "nudge this color's priority," an invisible effect. Now it means one thing — place this color in this seat. Explicit placement becomes the only steering mechanism, which is a simplification, but it does mean the board has to carry all the weight that ordering used to share.

Worth naming: this is the second concept you've cut for being confusing, after weight. The pattern seems to be that implicit, continuous signals lose to explicit, discrete ones here.

the bench, collapsed

A pill showing the count and the actual colors as dots, so even collapsed it says something is parked here. It expands on click, and it's a drop target while collapsed — drag a slot color onto the bar and it takes it.

One consequence to watch: with the bench closed, a color you add that doesn't win a seat lands somewhere invisible. In the live panel the bar pulses when that happens; a stronger option is auto-expanding on the first bench arrival per session.

chart as a series tray

chartCandidateIndexes is an array — several colors hold chart at once, so it can't be a slot. It's a wide tray below the board that accepts N colors, with smith-supplied entries dashed like everything else. It also usefully absorbs extra colors when someone arrives with eight.

the granularity test — read column 2

Column 2 is tweakcn's grouping rendered as slots, on the same code path. Two things show up immediately that a spec argument wouldn't have caught:

  • Most of the labels are token names, not intents. popover, muted, border & input, base — you can't guess any of them without already knowing shadcn. That's the exact assumption the board was invented to remove, reintroduced ten times over. The glosses get thinner too, because there's nothing to say beyond restating the token.
  • Three colors can't fill ten slots. Column 1 boots 3 yours / 3 derived. Column 2 boots 3 yours / 7 derived — the board stops reading as "your palette, arranged" and starts reading as "a form you haven't finished." Riff makes it worse: seven slots thrash at once.

And it still isn't tweakcn parity — tweakcn gives every group a background and a foreground, so honest parity is ~22 controls, not 10. At that point it isn't a generator, it's a token editor with extra steps.

the surface control (column 1)

The three gaps were real but they were one concept, not five. Three intent levers, sitting under the board:

separationflat / layered / lifted — how far apart the surfaces sit. lifted is what splits popover off card.
tintnone / subtle / strong — how much the greys borrow the primary hue. strong is what makes secondary buttons brand-tinted.
sidebarmatch / recessed / tinted — the one surface people most often want to break out.

Every one of them is a question about the design, not a token name — so it survives the test column 2 fails. And the live preview above them does the teaching, the same way the role tooltips do: show the effect, never name the variable.

trySet separation to lifted and watch the popover break away from the card. Then tint to strong and watch the secondary button pick up the brand. Those are the two intents the 6-role board genuinely couldn't express — now both are one click, and neither cost a slot.

Measured against the code: neutral drives 15 of the 44 app tokens and already yields 5 distinct surface values out of 8, so the ramp was never the problem — the missing piece was a way to aim it.

still open

  • Drag between two filled slots — currently a move (the source reverts to the smith). Swap might be less destructive but harder to predict. Genuinely unsure.
  • Height. The board + tray + collapsed bench is ~300px, which fits, but it pushes the dial below the fold on short viewports. The dial was the thing we promoted two rounds ago — worth checking it isn't being demoted by accident.
  • Where does the palette-as-a-set reading go? Chips gave you your colors as one coherent object. Sorted into slots, that's weakened. Maybe the collapsed bench bar grows into a full palette strip.
  • Does "lifted" need a real elevation model? Right now it lightens the popover; a proper implementation probably wants shadow tokens too, which themesmith doesn't generate at all yet.
  • Six glosses to write. The job lists derive themselves, but the one-line plain-English glosses are hand-written and will be read by every new user — worth drafting carefully rather than in a mockup.
