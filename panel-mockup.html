how to read this sheet

Every panel is wired to the same state machine — riff, lock, drag-reorder, remove/undo, the two-page start-over menu, the dial, export. Click through current first, then the same sequence in A. The markup barely changed; what changed is that the app now acknowledges you.

tryTurn on slow-mo 4× and hit riff in each column. In current, three colors teleport. In A and B, the three invented chips lift and cross-fade while your pinned rows visibly hold still — the motion is the explanation that riff only touches invented material. That's the one animation I'd fight for.

A — conservative pass

  • One icon set. Every ⧉ ✕ ▾ ↻ ⚄ ‹ ● glyph → lucide at a single 14px optical size. Biggest single credibility win, ~zero risk.
  • The padlock stops floating. It joins a section lead row with auto-assign, so justify-content:space-between always has two children instead of one.
  • One left rail. Grip, swatch, and every row's first glyph centre now land on the same x.
  • Six stray hexes → tokens (--ink-bright, --accent-cool, --accent-warm, --accent-quiet). The dead commented-out line in .ghost goes.
  • Motion added, structure untouched.

B — restructured

  • Header / body / footer. Export + status are pinned to the bottom as a frosted footer — they're the terminal action, and today they float mid-column above a huge void. Only the middle scrolls; the footer's top shadow appears only once content is actually behind it.
  • Labeled sections replace all four <hr>s, in the uppercase/0.16em voice .frame-indicator already speaks. Free hierarchy in a language the app owns.
  • The palette object. Strip + invented + add-field merge into one bordered body with a spine — a 3px gradient sampled from your actual candidates. Three stacked things at three insets become one object.
  • The chrome wears your palette. Wordmark gradient, frame dot, spine, focus edges, and the panel's corner wash all derive from the colors you fed it. A color tool whose own UI is stone grey is a missed argument — and it re-tints on every edit, which is its own quiet feedback channel.
  • The dial. Promoted to the marquee control it is: 8px gradient track, ticks, a grabbable glowing thumb, and a readout that travels with it instead of sitting in a corner chip.

new tokens this needs

Plus a duration/easing set — --d-fast 120ms, --d-base 200ms, --d-slow 340ms, --ease cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.3,1) — so timing is tunable in tokens.css rather than sprinkled through component files. B also needs three live --pal-* vars written from the candidate list.

open questions

  • B is 720px tall by design (fixed footer). On short viewports the body scrolls — acceptable, or should the footer un-pin below some height?
  • The palette-tinted chrome is the boldest move here. It's also the one that could fight a garish palette. Want a strength cap, or is a whisper at 13% safe enough?
  • Does the spine read as meaningful, or as decoration? It's the one element I'm least sure earns its place.
  • Implementation snag found while building this: B's section-header menus live inside the scrolling body, and overflow-y:auto clips them. They fit today, but in React these should portal out (or the body needs overflow:visible with the scroll moved to a wrapper). Worth deciding before it's built.
