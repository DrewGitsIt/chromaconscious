# App/Website Color Practice: Why UI Color Is Samey, and How to Be Expressive Yet Accessible

Scope note: researched 2026-10-06. Evidence labels used throughout: **[evidence]** = measured study or primary documentation of a mechanism; **[vendor research]** = company-run studies, not peer reviewed; **[opinion]** = critique, practitioner commentary, or convention. Contrast ratios marked "computed" were calculated by this researcher with the WCAG 2 relative-luminance formula, not taken from a source.

## Causes of color sameness in UI

### Takeaway
Sameness has one well-documented mechanical cause and several opinion-level ones. The mechanical cause: AI generators sample the statistical centre of their training data, and that centre is Tailwind's indigo plus Inter on white. The opinion-level causes are shared component kits and design systems, risk-averse "blanding", and category colour conventions such as bank blue. No rigorous study measuring UI colour homogeneity turned up. The critiques are mostly practitioner essays.

### Cited Findings
- **AI "distributional convergence" [evidence: primary doc of mechanism].** Anthropic's Applied AI team (Rajasekaran, Wei, Bricken; 12 Nov 2025) says that unguided landing pages "will almost always conform to Inter fonts, purple gradients on white backgrounds, and minimal animations." Their explanation: "Safe design choices–those that work universally and offend no one–dominate web training data. Without direction, Claude samples from this high-probability center." — [Anthropic: Improving frontend design through Skills](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)
- The skill's colour guidance: "Commit to a cohesive aesthetic. Use CSS variables for consistency." "Dominant colors with sharp accents outperform timid, evenly-distributed palettes." "Draw from IDE themes and cultural aesthetics for inspiration." "Vary between light and dark themes." Note that "outperform" is asserted, not measured. — [Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)
- The skill admits residual convergence: "You still tend to converge on common choices (Space Grotesk, for example) across generations." A later Anthropic harness post grades an "Originality" criterion under which "purple gradients over white cards" fail. — [wmedia.es summary](https://wmedia.es/en/tips/claude-code-frontend-design-skill); [Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- **The indigo origin story [primary statement, half-joking].** On 7 Aug 2025, the day GPT-5 launched, Adam Wathan posted: "I'd like to formally apologize for making every button in Tailwind UI `bg-indigo-500` five years ago, leading to every AI generated UI on earth also being indigo." It got roughly 1.5M views. — [Adam Wathan on X](https://x.com/adamwathan/status/1953510802159219096); [DEV: Blame Tailwind's indigo-500](https://dev.to/alanwest/why-every-ai-built-website-looks-the-same-blame-tailwinds-indigo-500-3h2p)
- One post argues the purple doesn't come from component libraries: "shadcn ships grey". It calls the purple "a decision nobody made". — [codemyspec: Why vibe coded websites all look the same](https://codemyspec.com/blog/vibe-coded-websites-look-the-same)
- **The "second-order slop" defaults [opinion, 2026].** Once purple became a known tell, a new set of "tasteful" defaults took over: cream + terracotta, monochrome, and Space Grotesk / Geist / Instrument Serif / Fraunces. The avoid-ai-design audit tool flags these. — [GitHub: funboy322/avoid-ai-design](https://github.com/funboy322/avoid-ai-design)
- Commonly listed AI-slop tells: a purple-to-blue gradient, Inter everywhere, a centred hero with three rounded feature cards, a 1px grey border on every card, an unrequested dark mode, glassmorphism with neon glow, uniform radius and padding. — [smoothui.dev](https://smoothui.dev/blog/ai-design-slop); [925studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells); [Mania Design](https://www.mania.design/blog/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designers-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults/)
- "AI slop" was the Macquarie Dictionary's 2025 word of the year. This is a secondary claim relayed by a design blog and was not verified at Macquarie. — [925studios guide](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-web-design-guide)
- **Pre-AI sameness [opinion].** Practitioner essays cite shared UI kits and Figma community files, website-builder templates (Webflow/Wix/Squarespace), minimalism plus responsive constraints, and "global design norms" copied from tech giants. One studio: designers "optimizing for safety... safety produces sameness." — [creativethatfits.com](https://creativethatfits.com/2025/03/why-do-modern-websites-look-the-same-today/); [rockpaperscissors.studio](https://rockpaperscissors.studio/why-everything-looks-the-same-in-2025-the-copy-paste-world/)
- **Blanding [opinion, with one quantitative attempt].** Origin of the critique: James Edmondson's 2018 "Everybody fall in line" tweet about tech brands converging on vanilla sans-serif logotypes, and Brunfaut/Greenwood's "army of clones wears a uniform of brand camouflage". Marker attempted a USPTO-data measurement using a Herfindahl index of abstract logo elements. Its conclusion wasn't visible in the snippet and the piece is about 5 years old. — [Marker: Are Brands Really Turning Into Blands?](https://marker.medium.com/are-brands-turning-into-blands-96158715ffbe)
- Blanding critics also raise an accessibility point: simplified rebrands lose contrasting colours and differentiation between icons. — [Barrett Media, Jul 2025](https://barrettmedia.com/2025/07/10/blanding-the-trend-thats-erasing-brand-identity/)
- **Category conventions (the corporate-blue trap) [opinion].** Traditional banks use blue, navy and grey to signal security, while fintechs use brighter colours. Purple has since become the new fintech cliché (Ally, Circle, Zelle), which reduces its distinctiveness. — [Tearsheet on Nubank](https://tearsheet.co/banking/nubank-intends-to-paint-the-us-banking-system-purple/); [patrickhuijs.com fintech colour guide](https://www.patrickhuijs.com/blog/fintech-brand-colors-guide)
- **Counter-argument [opinion].** Familiar patterns lower cognitive load and switching costs between products. — [Wendy Puan, Medium](https://medium.com/@wendypuanjingying/why-are-websites-starting-to-look-the-same-6e0ed789d2ee)

### Inferences
- For an agent-facing palette generator, the strongest lever is that the model never has to pick a colour from its prior. Every hue, chroma and neutral-tint decision should come from an explicit brief or a seeded generator. Banning purple alone just moves convergence to the next mode (cream/terracotta, monochrome), as the 2026 audit tools show. Distinctiveness probably has to be measured *relative to a reference set of common defaults*, not just checked against a blacklist.
- Tailwind's default neutrals (slate/gray/zinc) plus indigo/blue-500 at roughly 10% coverage is likely the modal "app look". A generator could compute distance from that point in OKLCH (hue of the accent, chroma of neutrals) as a "sameness score". This is a design suggestion, not sourced.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study that quantifies colour homogeneity across websites or apps over time. The claims are anecdotal or vendor-side.
- I found no reliable evidence that dark mode is now the default, and no data on how often it is shipped unrequested. It appears in slop-tell lists only as opinion.
- Flat design's specific contribution to colour sameness wasn't separately sourced.

## How design systems structure color

### Takeaway
Mature systems converge on the same architecture: a perceptually uniform tonal scale per hue, plus role-based semantic tokens (surface/layer, on-surface text, accent/brand, status), plus contrast guaranteed by lightness distance rather than per-pair checking. They differ in how much brand expression they allow, from Carbon's grey layering to Material 3 Expressive's deliberately emotive colour.

### Cited Findings
- **Material 3 / HCT [primary doc].** HCT = hue, chroma, tone. Tone equals CIELAB L*, and hue and chroma come from CAM16. At HSL "lightness" 50, measured lightness ranges from 33 to 96, which is why HSL is unreliable. A tone difference of 40 guarantees WCAG ≥3:1, and a difference of 50 guarantees ≥4.5:1. — [M3 blog: The science of color & design](https://m3.material.io/blog/science-of-color-design/); [M3 Glossary](https://m3.material.io/foundations/glossary)
- **Dynamic color [primary doc].** One source colour (wallpaper or content) produces five key colours (primary, secondary, tertiary, neutral, neutral-variant), each with a 0–100 tonal palette, which are then mapped to roles. Material Color Utilities is open source (`@material/material-color-utilities`). — [M3: How the system works](https://m3.material.io/styles/color/system/how-the-system-works)
- **Material 3 Expressive (I/O, May 2025) [vendor research].** Google cites 46 studies with 18,000+ participants over 3 years. Expressive designs were rated higher on "energetic, emotive, positive vibe, creative, playful, friendly". Users found key UI elements "up to 4x faster". 87% of 18–24-year-olds preferred the expressive designs. Perceived modernity rose 34% and boldness 30%. Google also states that expressive design "is not a solution for all situations". The methods were eye tracking, surveys, focus groups and usability tests. These figures cover colour together with shape, size, motion and containment, so colour's share isn't isolated. — [Android Developers: Benefits of expressive design](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/get-started/benefits); [Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/en/2025/05-1tp-3tea/15/material-3-expressive/)
- **Radix Colors [primary doc].** Each hue has a 12-step scale with fixed jobs: 1 = app background; 2 = subtle background; 3–5 = component background (normal/hover/pressed); 6–8 = borders (subtle → strong/focus); 9–10 = solid fill (step 9 has the highest chroma) and its hover; 11 = low-contrast text; 12 = high-contrast text. Steps 11 and 12 are "guaranteed to Lc 60 and Lc 90 APCA" on step 2 of the same scale. Most step-9 colours take white text, but Sky, Mint, Lime, Yellow and Amber need dark text. — [Radix: Understanding the scale](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale)
- **Tailwind v4 [primary doc].** The default palette moved from RGB to OKLCH, making it "more vivid" by using P3 gamut while keeping v3's balance. It has 22 families (5 neutrals, 17 hues) of 11 shades each (50–950), e.g. `--color-blue-500: oklch(62.3% 0.214 259.815)`. Custom colours are declared via `@theme`. — [Tailwind CSS v4.0](https://tailwindcss.com/blog/tailwindcss-v4)
- **IBM Carbon [primary doc].** Tokens are role-based and themes assign the values. There are 4 themes (White, Gray 10, Gray 90, Gray 100). Token groups: background, layer, field, border, text, link, icon, support (status), focus, skeleton. In the layering model, light themes alternate White/Gray 10 per layer and dark themes step one grade lighter per layer (layer-01…03). — [Carbon: Color overview](https://carbondesignsystem.com/guidelines/color/overview/)
- **Atlassian [primary doc].** Token grammar is `color.[property].[role].[emphasis].[state]`, e.g. `color.background.danger.bold.hovered`. A separate *accent* group (10 hues × subtlest/subtler/subtle/bold) handles colour that carries no meaning. The test for using one: "consider whether the color could be swapped for another without changing the experience or meaning." The pairing rules are explicit, e.g. inverse text on bolder accent backgrounds. — [Atlassian: Accents](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color/accents/); [Atlassian: Color](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color)
- **Apple HIG [primary doc, via search summary].** Apple calls for "judicious use of color". A single accent can mark interactivity (yellow in Notes, red in Calendar), and "other colors in your app shouldn't compete with it". Each accent should have light and dark variants. System colours adapt to Increase Contrast, and each tint has a high-contrast version. Don't rely on colour alone. — [Apple HIG: Color](https://developers.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/foundations/color/); [WWDC19 What's New in iOS Design](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/808/)
- **Stripe [primary, 2019].** Stripe found HSL unusable because blues look dark and yellows/greens look light at equal values. They moved to CIELAB so that hand-picked vibrant hues still met contrast, and every default text colour passed AA on white and on the lightest tints. The second claim comes from a secondary source. A later Stripe post (Ström-Awn) says the system was "stretched to the limit" and needed regeneration. — [Stripe: Designing accessible color systems](https://stripe.com/blog/accessible-color-systems); [Matt Ström-Awn: Generating color palettes](https://mattstromawn.com/writing/generating-color-palettes/)
- **Linear [primary doc].** Theme generation moved from HSL to LCH. It reduced "98 specific variables" per theme to 3 inputs: **base colour, accent colour, contrast**. LCH also drives elevation surfaces (background, panels, dialogs, modals). In the refresh, Linear "limit[ed] how much chrome (blue in their case)" tinted the neutrals, for "a more neutral and timeless appearance". The import format is CIE LCH JSON, e.g. `{"base":[96.11,9.98,77.81],"accent":[70.88,79.22,71.19],"contrast":20}`, which gives a warm cream with a gold accent; this comes from a third-party issue. — [Linear: How we redesigned the Linear UI (part II)](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui); [black-atom issue #29](https://github.com/black-atom-industries/black-atom/issues/29)

### Inferences
- Linear's 3-input model (base, accent, contrast) and Material's single-seed model show that a small number of perceptual inputs can deterministically produce a full accessible role set. That is directly analogous to what an agent-facing API should accept.
- Atlassian's "swap test" is a useful agent rule. If a colour carries meaning, it belongs in a semantic role with a fixed hue family. If it doesn't, it is free expressive territory (tags, illustration, surfaces), and that is where distinctiveness can be spent safely.
- HCT's tone-difference rule (Δ40 → 3:1, Δ50 → 4.5:1) works for WCAG 2. Radix's APCA Lc guarantees show the same "lightness-step contract" approach works for APCA.

### Gaps
- Adobe Spectrum's colour architecture and Material 3 Expressive's specific colour-token changes (versus shape/motion) weren't fetched.
- Apple HIG content came via a search summary, not a direct fetch.

## Distinctive, well-regarded product palettes

### Takeaway
Distinctive products seldom use more colour everywhere. They own one ownable signature (an unexpected hue for the category, or a signature mechanic like Spotify's artwork-derived colour). They keep neutrals tinted and disciplined, and they back colour with typography, illustration and naming. Several signature colours (Duolingo green, Mailchimp/Kakao yellow) fail text contrast on white, so they are used as fills and surfaces, with dark ink for text.

### Cited Findings
- **Duolingo.** Its primary is Feather Green #58CC02 ("When in doubt, lean into green"), with Mask Green #89E219, Eel #4B4B4B for text instead of black, and Snow (white) background. Accents are named after animals (Macaw #1CB0F6, Cardinal #FF4B4B, Bee #FFC800, Fox #FF9600, Beetle #CE82FF, Humpback #2B70C9). Illustration guidance: light pastels on white, never grey, which "looks lifeless". It uses a custom Feather Bold typeface. The guidelines are at design.duolingo.com; values here come via secondary summaries. — [Canny: Duolingo brand breakdown](https://www.canny-creative.com/brand-breakdown/brand/duolingo-a-brand-breakdown/); [duolingo-ux-reference color.md](https://github.com/virgiliojr94/duolingo-ux-reference/blob/master/references/color.md)
  - Computed: Feather Green on white is 2.09:1 (fails text even at large size), Eel on white is 8.72:1, Tree #58A700 on white is 3.02:1, and Cardinal #FF4B4B on white is 3.30:1. The brand hue therefore works as a button and surface fill, with the text role handed to a dark neutral.
- **Mailchimp (Collins, 2018).** Cavendish Yellow #FFE01B ("sunshine and optimism") is paired with near-black Peppercorn #241C15, plus food-named supporting colours (Dragonfruit, Radish). It uses the Cooper Light typeface and rough hand-drawn illustration. The rebrand broke with the then near-universal SaaS blue. — [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/mailchimp-goes-yellow-in-rebrand-by-collins/); [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/mailchimp-collins-brand-system-graphic-design-270918)
  - Computed: Cavendish on Peppercorn is 12.72:1, while Cavendish on white is 1.32:1. The signature works by putting dark text on the yellow surface.
- **Nubank.** Purple was chosen deliberately as "the most anti-bank color", against the blues and reds of Brazilian banks. Pentagram's 2021 rebrand kept the purple but expanded the palette to express "Latin American DNA". — [Tearsheet](https://tearsheet.co/banking/nubank-intends-to-paint-the-us-banking-system-purple/); [Pentagram: Nubank](https://www.pentagram.com/work/nubank)
- **KakaoTalk (Korea).** Yellow #FEE500 was chosen to differ from rivals (Naver green, Nate red, Daum blue, Facebook/Twitter blue). It began functionally, marking *your* chat bubble versus the other person's. Kakao T uses the yellow "sparingly and never as a full background". — [Design Compass: Kakao](https://designcompass.org/en/brands/kaleidoscope-14/); [oh-my-design: Kakao T](https://oh-my-design.kr/design-systems/kakaot)
- **M-Pesa / Safaricom (Kenya).** The palette is green and red, kept through the 2019 logo refresh. A secondary description reads them as money (green) plus urgency (red). — [Innovation Village](https://innovation-village.com/safaricom-refreshes-mpesa-brand-with-new-logo/); [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2019/10/07/safaricom-teases-refreshed-m-pesa-logo/)
- **Spotify.** The app sits on a dark #121212 base so that album art supplies the colour. Album and playlist pages compute a gradient from the artwork's dominant colour. Third-party analyses describe a pipeline of extraction, vibrancy filter, contrast check and gradient; Spotify hasn't confirmed its method. Semantic colours stay muted so they don't compete. Wrapped went from blood red, neon pink and canary yellow in 2024 to a reduced black/white/green/red in 2025 ("stripped down... simple and super modern" – J. Wirth). — [blakecrosley.com Spotify guide](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/spotify); [Envato on Wrapped 2025](https://elements.envato.com/learn/spotify-wrapped-design-aesthetic); [It's Nice That on Wrapped 2024](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/spotify-wrapped-2024-graphic-design-041224)
- **Linear.** See the design-systems section: near-achromatic tinted neutrals plus one indigo-violet accent. Unofficial hexes are #5e6ad2 and #7170ff; #5e6ad2 on white computes to 4.70:1. Note that Linear's accent is itself close to the "AI indigo" cliché, so its distinctiveness comes from neutral discipline, density and typography rather than hue. — [OpenDesign Linear write-up (unofficial)](https://open-design.ai/plugins/design-system-linear-app/); [Linear blog](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)

### Inferences
- There are two repeatable patterns. (a) *Ownable hue relative to the category*: Nubank against banks, Kakao against Korean portals, Mailchimp against SaaS blue. Distinctiveness is relational, so a generator needs the domain's incumbent hues as input. (b) *Signature mechanic*: Spotify's content-derived colour and Kakao's "your bubble" yellow, where colour is tied to a function and not just decoration.
- High-luminance signature hues (yellow, lime, bright green) almost always need a dark "ink" partner (Peppercorn, Eel, black). Colourblind-safe expressiveness can lean on this: a light, saturated surface with dark text gives large lightness contrast regardless of hue perception.
- Naming colours (animals, foods) is part of the brand system at Duolingo and Mailchimp. That is cheap for an agent to emulate and helps humans reason about roles.

### Gaps
- No primary sources were gathered for Stripe's current brand gradient, Notion, Figma, Arc, Teenage Engineering, Monzo (hot coral), Headspace or Airbnb. Search budget ran out. Treat any claims about them as unverified.
- LINE's official brand green wasn't found (a search summary suggested #06C755 from background knowledge, unverified). I found no Latin American or African example beyond Nubank and M-Pesa with design-team documentation.

## Proportion and placement

### Takeaway
"Use colour sparingly, with one dominant accent on interactive or primary elements" is consistent across Apple HIG, Kakao T and Spotify, and is reinforced by Material's guidance. The specific 60-30-10 ratio is an untested interior-design convention with no traceable origin. Treat it as a heuristic, not evidence.

### Cited Findings
- No source traces 60-30-10 to a person, publication or study. Explanations cite interior design (walls/furniture/accessories) or a dubious "Golden Section" link: 60:30 is 2:1, not 1.618. One SaaS guide even writes 60/30/20, which sums to 110%. — [Apartment Therapy](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/interior-design-rule-60-30-10-explained-37504313); [letsgroto](https://www.letsgroto.com/blog/60-30-10-rule)
- A third-party blog attributes a 60-30-10 framing to NN/g's Kelley Gordon. Unverified; I didn't find the NN/g article itself. — [sixtythirtyten blog](https://www.sixtythirtyten.co/blog/color-psychology-60-30-10-digital-products)
- Interior designers now call the rule a starting point and cite "colour drenching" (one colour everywhere) as a sophisticated alternative. — [Livingetc 2026](https://www.livingetc.com/advice/is-the-60-30-10-rule-outdated)
- Apple: one accent for interactivity, with which "other colors... shouldn't compete". — [Apple HIG: Color](https://developers.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/foundations/color/)
- Anthropic's skill: "Dominant colors with sharp accents outperform timid, evenly-distributed palettes" [opinion]. — [Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)
- Material 3 Expressive claims faster identification of key elements ("up to 4x") partly via colour and containment emphasis on primary actions [vendor research]. — [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/get-started/benefits)
- Coloured surfaces vs a white canvas: Mailchimp and Duolingo use white or neutral canvases with large signature-colour fields and illustrations. Spotify uses a dark neutral canvas and lets content supply the colour. Kakao T keeps its signature off full backgrounds. — sources above.

### Inferences
- A defensible rule set for agents: one neutral family (tinted) covering most area; one signature hue reserved for primary action, selection and brand moments; semantic hues only where they carry meaning; expressive accent hues allowed in no-meaning slots (tags, illustration, charts). The proportions should vary with domain and density rather than follow fixed percentages.
- "Bold" can come from surface colour (brand-tinted large fields) rather than more accent hues. A big, light, saturated surface with dark ink is expressive and keeps a lightness-based contrast story.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled study of the effect of accent coverage percentage on usability or brand recall in UI.

## Expressive color plus accessibility

### Takeaway
WCAG 2.x (4.5:1 / 3:1) is still the legal baseline (Section 508, EAA). APCA (a WCAG 3 draft candidate) fixes known errors: it passes some readable mid-tone pairs such as white on orange that WCAG 2 fails, and it fails dark-mode pairs that WCAG 2 passes. Colourblind-safe expressiveness comes from lightness contrast and redundant encoding (icons, text, shape), not from avoiding hue. Red/green semantics are allowed if they never stand alone.

### Cited Findings
- **APCA's "orange problem".** White on a sample orange scores APCA Lc 61 (readable at suitable size) but WCAG 2.97:1 (fail). Somers says it is "even more readable for individuals with CVD". A darker sample passes WCAG at 4.5:1 but scores APCA Lc 31, which is "half the contrast needed". WCAG 2 also "overstates contrast for dark colors", so 4.5:1 can be unreadable near black and "cannot be used for guidance designing dark mode". — [Myndex: WCAG 2 vs APCA](https://gist.github.com/Myndex/069a4079b0de2930e72d5401bde9af98); [APCA Easy Intro](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCAeasyIntro.html); [APCA in a Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)
- APCA returns signed Lc (about −108 to +106), polarity-aware. |Lc| ≥ 75 is for body text, and Lc 60/90 are used by Radix. One tool claims the two methods disagree on roughly 1 in 5 real-world pairs; that is an unverified vendor figure. Bridge-PCA is APCA-based but WCAG-2 backward compatible. — [humbldesign 2026 guide](https://humbldesign.io/blog-posts/color-accessibility-guide-wcag); [Radix scale](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale)
- **Practitioner consensus [opinion]:** pass WCAG 2.2 AA for audits and use APCA for perceptual tuning. On orange buttons, use dark label text. — [humbldesign](https://humbldesign.io/blog-posts/color-accessibility-guide-wcag)
- **WCAG SC 1.4.1 (Level A):** colour must not be the only visual means of conveying information. Fix it by adding a second channel (text label, icon with a *different shape*, border style), not by removing colour. Two same-shape dots in different colours isn't enough. — [Baseline: from a colourblind designer](https://baselinehq.com/blog/colourblindness-information-ui-design-red-green-problems-tips-tricks.html); [DEV: 8% of male users](https://dev.to/alphashark/8-of-your-male-users-cant-see-your-red-error-states-design-for-color-blindness-2ofa)
- Deutan/protan users see red and green as similar brown/grey. Differentiate semantic hues by **lightness**, and prefer the blue–orange axis, which red-green deficiency leaves intact. A colourblind designer recommends distinguishing primary from secondary buttons by style (solid vs outline) rather than green vs red. — [Baseline](https://baselinehq.com/blog/colourblindness-information-ui-design-red-green-problems-tips-tricks.html); [visualisingdata](https://visualisingdata.com/2019/08/five-ways-to-design-for-red-green-colour-blindness/)
- **[evidence]** The CatPAW study (arXiv 2602.06792, 2026) found redundant colour+shape encoding most beneficial at moderate complexity (5–8 categories). — [arXiv: CatPAW](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06792)
- Apple: if colour carries meaning, add text labels or glyph shapes, and give tints light, dark and high-contrast variants. — [Apple HIG](https://developers.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/foundations/color/)
- **Dark mode (Material 2 guidance) [primary doc].** Use dark grey #121212, not black, so shadows and elevation stay visible; Apple allows #000000. Desaturate primaries (around tone "200"), because saturated colours "vibrate" on dark backgrounds. Express elevation with lighter overlays (0% → 16% white at 24dp; 4dp ≈ #272727). Optionally tint the base with a low-opacity brand colour. M3 replaced the white overlays with tone-based surfaces and primary-tinted elevation. — [Material 2: Dark theme](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html)
- Bright saturated text on near-black can pass WCAG yet cause halation and eye strain [opinion]. — [APCA vs WCAG summary via humbldesign](https://humbldesign.io/blog-posts/color-accessibility-guide-wcag)

### Inferences
- For a deutan-first generator, the safe expressive envelope looks like this: put the distinctiveness budget into *chroma and hue of large surfaces and accents*, and guarantee all meaning-bearing distinctions via *lightness steps* (HCT/Radix style) and non-colour channels. Semantic success/error pairs should differ in OKLCH L by a meaningful margin, not only in hue, and should ship with icon and label pairing as part of the token contract.
- Running both metrics (WCAG 2 for compliance, APCA for dark mode and mid-tone fills) lets a generator legitimately use vivid mid-tone brand fills with dark text. That is exactly where signature colours like Kakao and Mailchimp yellow or Duolingo green live.

### Gaps
- WCAG 3's current status in 2026 wasn't confirmed: whether APCA is still the candidate and the latest draft date.
- I found no study measuring deutan users' discrimination of specific UI semantic colour pairs (versus chart palettes).

## Matching palette to user needs

### Takeaway
The evidence base is colour-emotion research. Its consistent finding is that **chroma and lightness drive affect more than hue**: high chroma means arousal or energy, light plus high chroma means positive or happy, low chroma means calm. Cross-cultural hue effects are smaller than commonly assumed. Practice-level elicitation (brand attributes, audience, domain conventions) is documented mostly as method and case study, not evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **[evidence]** Saturated, bright colours are associated with higher arousal, and arousal increases from blue and green toward red (Wilms & Oberfeld 2018, n=62). — [Wilms & Oberfeld 2018 PDF](https://www.staff.uni-mainz.de/oberfeld/downloads/Wilms-Oberfeld2018_Article_ColorAndEmotionEffectsOfHueSat.pdf)
- **[evidence]** A systematic review of 128 years of research found that saturated colours correspond to positive, high-arousal, high-power emotions, and desaturated colours to negative, low-arousal, low-power emotions. — [Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-024-02615-z)
- **[evidence]** In palettes designed for emotions, exciting, playful and positive palettes had high chroma and calm palettes had low chroma. Calm went with cooler hues and exciting with warmer ones. Cross-cultural work found chroma and lightness most important, with hue and cultural background effects "very limited". Lightness's effect on arousal is contested across studies. — summarised in [Frontiers in Psychology 2025 (Alvarado)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1593928/full); [PMC copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12202424/); [arXiv: Affective color scales](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14009)
- **Elicitation method [NN/g, method doc].** NN/g describes four ways to assess reactions to visual design: preference explanation, open and closed word choice (brand-attribute desirability), and numerical ratings for brand attributes. It recommends measuring both aesthetic impressions and behavioural effects. — [NN/g: Assessing user reactions to visual design](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/assessing-user-reactions-visual-design/)
- Google's M3 Expressive process is a worked example: designs were iterated against target attribute words ("playful, energetic, creative, friendly, positive vibe"), and Google cautioned that expressiveness doesn't fit every context [vendor research]. — [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/get-started/benefits)
- **Domain conventions [opinion].** Finance traditionally uses blue, navy and grey for trust, and fintechs differentiate with vivid hues. Risk: "If every major bank uses blue and you do too, your brand will feel 'right'... radically different... risk feeling out of place." — [patrickhuijs fintech guide](https://www.patrickhuijs.com/blog/fintech-brand-colors-guide); [BFA Global: fintech color](https://bfaglobal.com/catalyst-fund/insights/getting-your-fintech-brand-right-stand-out-with-color/)
- Fixes for AI sameness converge on making explicit brief decisions: "five decisions about theme, color, type, layout and tone, made once and written down" [opinion]. — [codemyspec](https://codemyspec.com/blog/vibe-coded-websites-look-the-same)

### Inferences
- An agent brief could map attribute words to OKLCH targets: energetic/playful → higher accent chroma and lighter values; calm/focused/long-session tools → low-chroma neutrals and a restrained accent; premium → low chroma, deep lightness, one precise accent. Hue is then chosen mostly for *category distinctiveness* (avoid the incumbents' hues) and cultural or domain meaning, since hue carries less emotional signal than chroma and lightness.
- Density and session length (dashboards, IDEs, finance tools) argue for low-chroma tinted neutrals and colour reserved for state. Short-session, consumer, kids or learning products (Duolingo) tolerate large saturated surfaces. This comes from the examples, not a study.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous source on how agencies translate brand attributes into specific colour values, and no evidence on domain conventions for health or kids beyond opinion.
- Cultural colour meanings in Asia, Africa and Latin America weren't researched beyond the Kakao, M-Pesa and Nubank cases.
