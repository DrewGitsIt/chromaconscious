the padlock flow

1An open padlock sits above the candidates. Unlocked is the default and means: full engine, hues invented freely. It's visible before you know the feature exists — that's the point of promoting it out of a menu. The "↺ reset pins" ghost shares this row but only appears when a pin exists (state 4, where rust is pinned to danger) — and the base is not a pin: reset pins never unlocks it.

2Click it and the strip becomes the menu. Pick-mode highlights every candidate row as a target ("click a color to lock its hue"); Esc, clicking outside, or the padlock again cancels. With exactly one candidate — the brand-color user, the common case — the prompt is skipped and the padlock locks it in one click.

3Locked state shows its captive. The padlock closes and the base swatch + hex move in next to it; the caption states the contract ("mono — engine invents no new hues"). The locked row keeps a ⬤ base badge in the strip, so the state reads in both places. Clicking the little swatch re-enters pick-mode to crown a different color.

4Unlock parks the base instead of forgetting it. The swatch stays next to the open padlock, dimmed; one click re-locks it. Flip, compare, flip back — no re-picking. Only removing the color from the strip clears the memory.

5Fidelity keeps its exact meaning. The dial still governs how much the engine may move your colors — there's just one axis left to move on. The caption switches vocabulary: ΔE becomes ΔL. At raw-colors the base's own lightness is kept verbatim; at smith's-taste it snaps to the ladder.

6Added colors are exempt — that's the workflow. The lock constrains what the engine synthesizes, never what you hand it. Aquamarine walks in through normal scoring (or a pin) and claims accent with its real hue; rust takes danger. Strict mono isn't a mode — it's the lock with nothing added yet. "Mono + functional color" isn't a mode either — it's the natural result of layering.

what locking means (engine)

The base donates hue + chroma to everything the engine invents — the tinted neutral ramp, the primary solid, every role without a user-supplied color, chart fills. Under the lock the engine varies lightness only: no hue rotation, no status hue anchors, no +45° chart spins.

· roles.ts — under lock, synthesis returns base-hue lightness variants instead of hue-anchored colors; fidelityAdjust stops injecting chroma (the clamp that would tint a locked gray).
· repair.ts — MOVES truncates to the two lightness moves for locked-provenance seeds; user-added colors keep the full move set.
· tokens.ts — chart synthesis switches from hue rotation to an even lightness ladder over the base.
· ramp.ts / contrast / report — untouched. The ladder, APCA text solving, and every check already operate in lightness.

the report under lock

Role assignment rows gain a provenance note — "synthesized · mono (lightness of base)" — so it's legible why success is salmon-tinted instead of green. The status chip and contrast checks run unchanged; mono themes tend to pass more easily, not less.

dropped from the last round

The badge-menu "⬤ mono base" item and the mono bar are gone — the padlock is the single way in, the single flip-switch, and the single status display. One concept, one control.
