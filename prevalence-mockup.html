1Candidate row v2 — the bar becomes a slider, order becomes just order

Today the value is welded to list position: dragging a color to a new slot swaps it into that slot's weight (plus a second hidden order bonus). In v2 the value belongs to the color: a real slider named prevalence, seeded by image population or add order, editable in place. Reordering the list moves nothing but the row — the flip you hit literally cannot happen from a drag anymore. Only the deliberate gesture (sliding) changes casting.

3Sliding across a cliff — the flip happens in front of you, then offers undo

Prevalence feeds a discrete optimizer, so the slider has flats and cliffs — that's physics we can't hide, only disclose. The theme already recomputes live: mid-drag, the chips flip in the sidebar and the mockup re-inks. On release, if any seat changed hands, the existing toast machinery names the trade and offers one undo.

Design notes & open questions

  1. Two levers, cleanly split: prevalence is evidence, pin is intent. The slider says “this is how much of this color I have”; the chip dropdown says “this is what I want it to be”. The accent rarity term survives untouched — under prevalence semantics it's correct design theory — but no gesture trips it by accident anymore, and when a deliberate slide does, state 3 discloses the trade.
  2. Reorder becomes purely organizational. Slot-welded weights and the hidden orderBonus both die; prevalence travels with the color. This deletes the original bug class (drag-up demotes) rather than explaining it better. Engine change is small: seed prevalence at add/extract time, stop recomputing from position.
  3. The engine finally shows its work. The “why” section in state 2 is generated from the actual scores (which gate failed, who won the contested seat, by how much) — not canned strings. Same data powers the near-miss nudge (“missed chart by a hair”), which quietly teaches the model.
  4. The accent audit is adapter work, not engine work. Casting already picks a good pop color; the app adapter just spends it on a hover wash because of the shadcn name collision. New jobs: links, inline actions, selected-tab indicator, focus ring, secondary button, first chart slot. Export stays fully shadcn-compatible — we're changing which tokens the mockup consumes and pointing ring at the accent ramp, not renaming tokens.
  5. Open question — does UNUSED deserve a rescue action? The pin-to-chart item in state 2 nudges chroma to qualify the color (0.041 → 0.05 is invisible to the eye). Alternative: leave unused colors truly untouched and let the pin force it verbatim, accepting a washed-out data series. I lean nudge-with-disclosure — it follows the fidelity philosophy (normalize at the seams, disclose the shift).
  6. Open question — slider footprint. Always-visible thumbs (mocked here) make the value obviously editable but add visual noise × N rows. Alternative: thumb appears on row hover, bar otherwise identical to today. I lean always-visible — discoverability is the whole point of this change.
  7. Not doing: score bars in the row. Considered showing each color's per-role scores on hover (full transparency). It turns the sidebar into an engine debugger; the one-sentence “why” plus the live flip covers 95% of the confusion at 5% of the pixels.
