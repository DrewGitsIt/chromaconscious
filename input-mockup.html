1First run — the stage is the invitation

No candidates → no theme to preview → the stage hosts the input methods, large and undistracted. The sidebar keeps only the brand; every control it would show is meaningless without colors anyway. The moment a first color/preset/image lands, the hero yields to the working layout (state 2). Presets become visible palette cards instead of a blind dropdown — they finally get to demo themselves.

2Working state — add lives in the strip, start-over lives at the top

The additive verb (add a color) becomes the last row of the candidate strip — it visually reads as “grow this list”. The destructive verbs (image / preset / clear) collapse into one small start over door next to the frame controls, at session level, far from the strip. The old always-visible input block is gone entirely.

Design notes & open questions

  1. The organizing principle: verbs live where they act. “Add a color” grows the candidate list → it renders inside the list, as its last row. “Start over” replaces the session’s colors → it sits at session level, next to the frame controls, physically far from the strip. The additive/destructive distinction you named becomes spatial instead of being flattened into one box.
  2. The hero breaks the “canvas is mockup-only” rule — deliberately and temporarily. When there are zero candidates there is no mockup to show; the hero occupies the void and self-destructs on first input. No control lives in the canvas while a theme exists.
  3. Open question — should the app still boot with Coastal starter? Today it does, so no user ever sees the empty state unless they hit Clear. Your “foot in the door” idea argues for booting empty into the hero: the first thing a user does is their choice, which teaches all three doors at once. The preset cards in the hero keep the show-something-instantly value. I lean boot-empty, but it changes first impressions and is worth deciding explicitly.
  4. No confirmation dialogs. Destructive verbs stay one-click; the drag overlay discloses before, the undo toast forgives after. Cheaper than a modal and doesn’t punish the 95% case where starting over is exactly what you meant.
  5. “Clear” disappears as a word. It becomes “start empty” inside the start-over menu — the only truly rare verb, now priced accordingly.
  6. Alternative considered: keep the add-color field above the strip (like today, minus dropzone and preset dropdown). Less novel, but the field then reads as a peer of the padlock rather than part of the collection. I prefer the in-strip row; easy to flip if it feels cramped with many candidates.
