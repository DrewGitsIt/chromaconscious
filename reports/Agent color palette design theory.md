# Ground eyeless agents in lightness, materials, and measurement

The best evidence says pleasing palettes are built mostly from **lightness and chroma structure**. Hue geometry matters much less. People reliably prefer one or two hue families, a wide light-to-dark ramp arranged as a gradient, an overall light palette, similar chroma within a tier, and a small accent that contrasts in hue with its ground. Light yellows sit high and blues sit low. The classic colour-wheel templates (complementary, triadic) mostly fail when tested against ratings. That matters for ChromaConscious because the same lightness-first structure that people like also passes WCAG/APCA and survives deutan vision. Aesthetics, contrast and colourblind safety mostly point the same way, and they really conflict only for categorical colours: chart series and status hues. Taken across cultures, perceptual dimensions and broad colour-emotion links are close to universal (r = .88 across 30 nations). Hue *preference*, harmony judgments and colourfulness tolerance vary by audience, so none of them should be hard-coded as one "good taste." Agents have no eyes, but they hold near-human colour-concept associations (r ≈ .71–.80). They are poor at measuring colour, and left alone they default to the statistical median (indigo on white, then cream and terracotta). They produce good palettes when (a) a deterministic tool does all measuring, (b) every hue decision comes from an explicit brief or a provenance-tagged reference (a scene, material, pigment or curated historical combination) rather than the model's prior, and (c) distinctiveness is measured against the known defaults. The recommendations at the end turn this into API feedback, a licensed reference library, a needs-elicitation script and anti-default rules.

A note on colour descriptions. This report gives hex values and OKLCH (L 0–1, C, h°) or CIELAB numbers next to colour names, so no claim depends on telling red from green by eye. The arguments that matter most here run along lightness and the blue–yellow axis, which deutan vision keeps.

## Lightness structure, not the colour wheel, predicts which palettes people like

Most classical colour theory was never derived from aesthetic measurement. A 2013 *Annual Review of Psychology* survey notes that **none of Chevreul, Itten, Munsell, Ostwald, Goethe or Moon & Spencer built their theories on aesthetic data** ([Palmer, Schloss & Sammartino 2013](https://palmerlab.berkeley.edu/pdf/PalmerSchlossSammartino(2013)AR.pdf)). When the hue-wheel templates were finally tested at scale, they failed. O'Donovan, Agarwala and Hertzmann analysed 104,426 Kuler themes with 327,381 ratings, 383,938 COLOURlovers themes, and 10,743 themes rated 40 times each on Mechanical Turk. They found "little evidence that people gravitate to templates naturally, or that matching a template produces higher scores." Themes close to templates actually scored slightly lower ([O'Donovan et al. 2011](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~donovan/color/colorcomp.pdf)). A packaging study that tested principles one by one found complementary pairs looked *disharmonious*. It concluded that only hue similarity, and hue similarity plus similar chroma, are tenable ([Wei et al., IJDesign](http://www.ijdesign.org/index.php/ijdesign/article/view/1130/612)).

What survives is a consistent cluster of findings. Schloss and Palmer's 992-pair figure–ground study shows that pair preference and harmony both rise with hue similarity. **Preference, but not harmony, also rises with lightness contrast.** Harmony is higher for lighter pairs and pairs of similar saturation. Harmony, the two single-colour preferences and the figure–ground lightness difference together explain more than 80% of pair-preference variance; single-colour preference alone explains 21.7% ([Schloss & Palmer 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3037488/)). Their shorthand for the effect is that people prefer "different shades of the same basic color (e.g., vivid blue on light blue) rather than different colors of similar shades." O'Donovan's learned model agrees: **mean lightness was the top feature on MTurk, while dark themes rated fine on Kuler if at least one colour was bright**, and every model penalised high lightness standard deviation. Together these favour "high contrast with low standard deviation, that is, a gradient." Designers preferred hue entropy equivalent to about **2–3 hue families**, while lay raters did not penalise monochrome but marked down palettes with many hues ([O'Donovan et al. 2011](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~donovan/color/colorcomp.pdf)).

A separate distinction resolves an apparent contradiction about complementary colours. *Figural* preference, meaning how much people like a colour shown on a coloured ground, **rises with hue contrast and lightness contrast against the ground**, even though the same pair is not rated harmonious ([Palmer et al. 2013](https://palmerlab.berkeley.edu/pdf/PalmerSchlossSammartino(2013)AR.pdf)). So a hue-contrasting call-to-action button on a harmonious field is well supported, while two large complementary areas side by side are not. The 2025/26 iScience study by Forni, Darmon and Benzaquen pushes back on the hue-similarity consensus. Its 346 respondents in France, Japan and Europe preferred near-opposite hues on average, matching hue statistics in 12,000 landscape photos ([arXiv 2508.15777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15777)). It held HSL lightness fixed at 0.5, though, and HSL lightness at 0.5 spans perceived lightness from about L* 33 to 96 ([Material 3](https://m3.material.io/blog/science-of-color-design/)). Its "complementary" pairs were therefore almost certainly also lightness-contrast pairs. That confound is consistent with the figural-preference finding rather than a refutation of it.

### A computable harmony model exists, with known blind spots

The most concrete equation is Ou & Luo's 2006 two-colour model in CIELAB. Harmony is the sum of a chromatic-difference term, a lightness term and a hue term, and multi-colour palettes are scored as the mean over all pairs. That averaging correlated r = 0.71 with observers on 6,545 colour wheels and r = 0.93 on 111 interior images ([Ou & Luo 2006](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/col.20208); [Ou et al. 2011](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/col.20624); equations restated in [US patent 9134179](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9134179)). Working through its tanh terms gives usable thresholds. These are the research team's arithmetic, not the authors' statements. The lightness-difference bonus saturates near **ΔL\* ≈ 20 and never penalises more**, so large ΔL for text contrast costs nothing. Summed lightness has its midpoint at a mean **L\* ≈ 67**, which penalises dark themes. The chromatic-difference term crosses zero at about 18 CIELAB units, so accents should stay near the base hue or low in chroma. Hue effects only switch on above about C\* 4–8, so very low-chroma tinted greys are effectively hue-neutral.

The model's yellow term encodes the **"natural order" of hues**: yellows belong light and blues and violets belong dark. Its sign flips at about L\* 58, so light yellows add harmony and dark yellows subtract. This matches a cross-cultural finding that **dark yellow (olive) and dark orange (brown) are strongly disliked** by Chinese, British, Japanese and American observers. One proposed reason is association with rot and waste ([Palmer et al. 2013](https://palmerlab.berkeley.edu/pdf/PalmerSchlossSammartino(2013)AR.pdf)). In forced choice, people also prefer **lighter, yellower figures on darker, bluer grounds**, across figure areas of 40–60% ([Schloss & Palmer, Perception 2011](https://doi.org/10.1068/p6992)). The natural order is also physical: in sRGB and P3 the highest-chroma yellows exist only at high lightness and the highest-chroma blues only at low lightness. Following it maximises the chroma available.

These models have clear limits. They were fitted on small panels (Ou & Luo's core experiment had about 17 observers, Szabó's about 9) from mostly UK and US universities. **They screened out colourblind observers.** They were fitted on swatches, not rendered interfaces, and none has been validated on a full UI theme. They are fitted in CIELAB, not Oklab. ChromaConscious would have to convert OKLCH to CIELAB before applying the coefficients. Ou's model also lost to a competitor on clothing combinations ([Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40691-025-00433-y)). The defensible use is as an advisory signal and as a source of structural rules: gradient lightness, few hue families, no dark yellow. It should not be an objective to maximise.

### Individual taste varies more than group averages suggest

Group averages are stable, but **pairwise agreement between Kuler users was only 52%, barely above chance**. Users fall into roughly ten taste clusters with 81% agreement within a cluster, and models trained separately on demographic groups barely helped: "personal preferences outweigh demographic preferences" ([O'Donovan et al. 2011](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~donovan/color/colorcomp.pdf)). Preference for harmony is itself a trait. Individual correlations between harmony and liking range from −0.73 to +0.97, and art training lowers preference for harmony ([Palmer et al. 2013](https://palmerlab.berkeley.edu/pdf/PalmerSchlossSammartino(2013)AR.pdf)). This is the strongest argument against a single optimum. A generator should let the user set *how harmonious versus how striking* they want the palette, rather than claim one right answer.

## Universal perception, local meaning: what holds across cultures

Colour-*emotion* associations are largely shared. Jonauskaite et al. surveyed 4,598 people in 30 nations and 22 languages. They found **mean cross-national similarity of r = .88**, yet a classifier still identified a participant's nation 34.4% of the time, and similarity tracked linguistic and geographic distance ([Psychological Science 2020 via HAL](https://hal.science/hal-03646867v1)). The documented local deviations cluster around mourning and religion. Chinese participants also linked white with sadness (white is worn at funerals), Greeks linked purple with sadness (Orthodox liturgical mourning), Nigerians also linked red with fear, and Egyptians did not link yellow with joy ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2020/09/200910150247.htm); [Jonauskaite 2019](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/abs/10.1098/rsos.190741)). A model pooled from 12 regions shows warm/cool, heavy/light and active/passive generalising well (r ≈ 0.78–0.81), while harmony generalises less well (r = 0.72). The authors conclude that colour emotion is "relatively more culture-independent" than colour harmony ([Ou et al. 2018](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/col.22243)).

Hue *preference* is the weakest universal. "Everyone likes blue" fits industrialised samples. Ecological valence explained about 80% of variance in Americans' preferences for 32 colours ([Palmer & Schloss 2010](https://escholarship.org/content/qt9fr2s1z1/qt9fr2s1z1_noSplash_852a660b6f43f17c2f472fba69568a3c.pdf)). The pattern breaks among the Himba of rural Namibia, who rated the "universal" blues low and saturated warm hues high ([Taylor, Clifford & Franklin 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233410106_Color_Preferences_Are_Not_Universal)). It fits Japanese and Arabic data less well ([Yokosawa et al. 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cogs.12291)). Across the affect literature, **chroma and lightness drive emotional response more than hue**: saturated colours read as positive, high-arousal and high-power, desaturated ones as low-arousal ([Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-024-02615-z)). Cross-cultural palette work finds hue and cultural background effects "very limited" next to chroma and lightness ([Frontiers in Psychology 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1593928/full)).

### Interface norms differ in colourfulness, not in a fixed regional hue

Measured web data contradicts the common stereotypes. Reinecke and Gajos collected about 2.4 million appeal ratings from about 40,000 people on 430 websites. **The colourfulness and complexity levels at which appeal peaks differ by country, gender and age.** Women preferred more colourful sites, and Macedonians liked highly colourful designs more than any other group. The authors question universal design guidelines ([Reinecke & Gajos, CHI 2014](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2014/reinecke14visual.pdf)). Japanese sites measure as high-complexity but *low* average saturation, against medium-complexity, highly saturated US sites ([CHI 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3411764.3445519)). A cross-national comparison found the highest saturation in Chile and Nigeria and the lowest in Saudi Arabia ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348017616_Culturally_Sensitive_Website_Elements_and_Features_A_Cross-National_Comparison_of_Websites_from_Selected_Countries)). Japanese respondents scored white/red portal backgrounds at 0.06 while UK and Chinese respondents rated red higher ([PLOS ONE 2023](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0282712)). That alone shows "East Asia" is not one category. Hofstede-based predictions for colour, such as Marcus & Gould's, often fail against measured data. The practical encoding is that **colourfulness and density are audience parameters, while hue is not determined by region**.

### Supported meanings versus folk glossaries

Only a narrow tier of cultural colour meaning has empirical or institutional backing. That tier covers white and mourning in China, purple and mourning in Greece, and context-bound institutional meanings: Māori red, black and white in the Tino Rangatiratanga flag ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tino_Rangatiratanga_flag)), the Javanese court sogan browns versus the coastal pesisir palettes ([Batik Guild](https://www.batikguild.org.uk/batik/indonesia)), and kente as royal regalia ([Miami University Art Museum](https://sites.miamioh.edu/art-museum/2019/02/kente-cloth-a-look-inside-40-at-40/)). The researchers could not trace the popular infographic claims to empirical sources: purple as mourning in Brazil or Thailand, yellow as mourning in Egypt (the data shows only a missing joy link), green as taboo in Islamic contexts, red as danger everywhere, blue as trust everywhere. Kente colour keys turn out to be "contextual, historically layered, and sometimes contradictory" ([Craft Atlas](https://craftatlas.co/crafts/kente)), and Aboriginal ochre meanings vary by Nation ([Mandel Art Gallery](https://mandelartgallery.com.au/blogs/mandel-art-gallery-blog/aboriginal-ochre-colours)). An agent that recites such glossaries as fact is stereotyping. It should present them, at most, as possible sensitivities to check with the audience.

## Materials and pigments ground colour better than nation labels

Asking an agent for an "Indian palette" invites cliché. Asking for colours grounded in a *material* points it at physics and documented practice. Several traditions come with authoritative structured data. **JIS Z 8102 defines 269 Japanese colour names in Munsell notation** (azuki-iro = 8R 4.5/4.5; JIS "red" converts to about #BE0032, not #FF0000) ([Nippon/CRAN](https://rdrr.io/cran/Nippon/man/JapaneseColors.html)). The Palace Museum's *Chinese Traditional Colors* organises 384 names by season and ties each to an artefact ([Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Chinese%20Traditional%20Colors:%20The%20Aesthetics%20of%20Colors%20in%20the%20Forbidden%20City/71682)). Most other traditions are documented through chemistry, and that chemistry is shared across them. Indigo underlies Andean, Indian, Javanese and Thai blues and Maya blue. Anthraquinone reds include cochineal, lac, chay/madder and morinda. Iron gives Falu red, Māori kōkōwai, Indian iron-tannin black and zellige browns. Glaze ions do the rest: cobalt blue, copper turquoise and green, tin white. Spectroscopy, HPLC or XRF exists for nearly all of these ([MDPI Heritage 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/heritage4030091); [Moroccan zellige PIXE](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263904416_PIXE_ANALYSIS_OF_MOROCCAN_ARCHITECTURAL_GLAZED_CERAMICS_OF_14TH-18TH_CENTURIES); [Heritage Science 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-015-0037-2)).

Two cautions follow from the chemistry. First, **hue depends on process and period, so one hex per "traditional colour" is a simplification.** Tyrian purple shifts with bath pH and debromination ([Structural Chemistry 2017 via Academia](https://www.academia.edu/103794741)). Old zellige green is a copper green while modern zellige uses chromium, a different chemistry and a different hue ([Bendaoud et al. 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S096980431300290X)). "Andean red = cochineal" is period-dependent, since some Wari pieces use only plant reds ([Heritage 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/heritage4030091)). Second, published hex values for these colours are mostly modern reinterpretations that disagree with each other. Sakura-iro is #FEEEED on one site and #FCC9B9 on another ([ColorFYI](https://colorfyi.com/blog/japanese-traditional-colors/); [Coloracci](https://coloracci.ai/blog/japanese-color-culture-traditions)). Any sRGB value describes a pigment on cloth, wood or glaze under daylight. It is an interpretation and should be labelled as one.

Several traditions also carry **structural rules** that map onto a theme engine better than their swatches do. Heian *kasane* are ordered three-layer seasonal combinations (outer, middle, lining), a natural fit for surface and elevation layers. Obangsaek and wuxing give five primaries plus intermediates, close to a five-slot categorical set. Haft-rang tilework puts colours on a white ground separated by black lines, which amounts to a high-contrast outline system ([Wikipedia: Haft-rang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haft-rang)). Bògòlan is a three-tone tannin and iron palette of ochre-brown, black and off-white ([Bowers Museum](https://collections.bowers.org/objects/144806/mud-cloth-bogolanfini?ctx=a511dcd296f5085a2c8c5dedfd9ddca227aa8d94&idx=4)). Barragán used saturated planes and left vegetation as the neutral ([ArchEyes](https://archeyes.com/casa-gilardi-a-masterpiece-of-color-and-light-by-luis-barragan/)). Lightness-separated sets such as kōwhaiwhai red/black/white and sogan brown over indigo are likely CVD-robust. Hue-separated sets at similar lightness, such as zellige blue and green or Thaitone's red/green pairs, need simulation checks.

### Nature supplies coherence through shared light

Natural scenes look coherent for physical reasons that can be written as rules. Every surface in a scene shares one illuminant, which gives all swatches a common hue bias. That bias plays the same role as the brand tint a designer adds to neutrals. **Aerial perspective lowers contrast and chroma with distance and pulls far colours toward the air's colour** ([Wikipedia: Aerial perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_perspective)). That maps directly onto UI layering: background layers low in chroma and air-tinted, foreground accents at full chroma. Blue hour gets its colour mostly from ozone (Chappuis) absorption, not Rayleigh scattering alone ([ESO Messenger](https://www.eso.org/sci/publications/messenger/archive/no.143-mar11/messenger-no143-27-31.pdf)). Autumn colour comes from three pigment families, the anthocyanin reds shifting toward purple as acidity falls ([USDA Forest Service](https://www.fs.usda.gov/visit/fall-colors/science-of-fall-colors)). Ecological valence adds a cheap trick: liking a colour tracks liking for the objects it evokes. Naming positive referents ("clear sky", "wet slate", "straw") rather than negative ones ("mud", "bile") likely helps at similar hues. That is an inference from Palmer and Schloss, not a tested claim. There is a gap here: **no study shows that nature-extracted palettes are rated more harmonious than designed ones**, and claims that they are "proven" are marketing ([example](https://www.higocreative.com/blog/nature-color-palettes)).

Material appearance in the perceptual sense (gloss, translucency) is not a colour property. The visual system reads gloss from the size, contrast and sharpness of highlights, and translucency from cues such as saturation and highlight structure ([Fleming 2014](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259109278_Visual_perception_of_materials_and_their_properties); [Gigilashvili et al. 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8340651/)). In flat UI, a material therefore becomes four colour parameters: a narrow lightness band, a chroma ceiling, an undertone hue, and a variation range. Its finish (matte, satin, gloss) belongs in elevation and shadow tokens. Material 3 does exactly this with tinted neutrals at about **chroma 4 (neutral) and 8 (neutral-variant)** in HCT ([dt.in.th](https://dt.in.th/M3DynamicColorJS)). Linear did it by tuning its default grey from a cool bluish tint to "a warmer gray … less saturated" ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)). All three 2026 paint-industry colours of the year are low-chroma neutrals, including Pantone's first-ever white, Cloud Dancer 11-4201 ([Pantone](https://www.pantone.com/color-of-the-year/2026)). They are better read as evidence that tinted neutrals are the most reusable material-derived layer than as trend data to copy.

## Agents know colour meaning but cannot measure it, and they drift to the median

Text-only LLMs carry a usable colour-concept map. Mukherjee, Mohapatra, Rogers and Schloss tested 70 concepts against 71 colours. **GPT-4 text-only reached mean r ≈ .71–.73 with human ratings, and GPT-4V with patches plus hex reached .80.** No model reached human split-half reliability of about .93. Bar-chart palettes built from the model's ratings were interpreted correctly 81% of the time ([Cognitive Science 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13286014/)). Accuracy falls for "low-specificity" concepts whose colour associations are spread flat ("shoes", "greed"). That predicts brief words like "modern", "professional" or "trustworthy" will give weak signals. ColorGPT found **hex codes are a strong colour representation for LLMs, and adding descriptive words improves accuracy further** ([arXiv 2508.08987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08987)). Perception is far weaker than semantics. Vision-language models name prototypical colours well but drop sharply on non-prototypical ones ([arXiv 2509.22524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22524)). One benchmark measured **exact text-colour accuracy of 6.3% over 59 colours**, and in 21.6% of cases the model reported the written colour word instead of the ink ([arXiv 2511.13400](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13400)). The division of labour is clear: the agent handles meaning, and the API handles measurement.

Left unguided, models sample the centre of their training data. Anthropic's own frontend-design guidance says unguided landing pages "will almost always conform to Inter fonts, purple gradients on white backgrounds," because "safe design choices … dominate web training data" ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). Tailwind's creator half-jokingly apologised for making every Tailwind UI button `bg-indigo-500` (#6366F1 in Tailwind v3), "leading to every AI generated UI on earth also being indigo" ([Adam Wathan](https://x.com/adamwathan/status/1953510802159219096)). A W4A 2025 study found "consistent use of blue color schemes" in ChatGPT- and Claude-generated banking UIs, and a 2026 review found insufficient contrast was the second most common LLM accessibility failure ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3744257.3744266); [arXiv 2605.13873](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.13873)). Banning purple only moves the mode. By 2026, audit tools flag a "second-order" default set: cream plus terracotta, monochrome, and Space Grotesk or Fraunces type ([avoid-ai-design](https://github.com/funboy322/avoid-ai-design)). Model architecture makes the same point. Colormind's regression model "avoided bright colors" and drifted to browns and greys because it was "afraid to be wrong," and switching to a GAN fixed this ([Colormind blog](http://colormind.io/blog/)). O'Donovan found good themes lie on a manifold near popular themes ([project page](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~donovan/color/)). **Typicality predicts crowd liking, and the same pull produces the generic look.** That is why distance from typical should be a deliberate setting the agent chooses, not an accident.

### Distinctive products own one thing and keep the rest disciplined

Well-regarded palettes rarely use more colour everywhere. They own one hue that is unusual *for their category* and keep their neutrals disciplined. Nubank chose purple as "the most anti-bank color" against the blues and reds of Brazilian banks ([Tearsheet](https://tearsheet.co/banking/nubank-intends-to-paint-the-us-banking-system-purple/)). KakaoTalk's yellow #FEE500 was picked against Naver, Nate and Daum, and is used "sparingly and never as a full background" ([Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/en/brands/kaleidoscope-14/)). Mailchimp's Cavendish Yellow #FFE01B broke with SaaS blue ([Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/mailchimp-goes-yellow-in-rebrand-by-collins/)). Distinctiveness is relational, so a generator needs the category's incumbent hues as an input. The pattern is also deutan-friendly. These high-luminance signatures fail text contrast on white: by the research team's WCAG computations, Cavendish on white is 1.32:1 and Duolingo's Feather Green #58CC02 on white is 2.09:1. They work as **light, saturated surfaces carrying dark ink**: Cavendish on Peppercorn #241C15 is 12.72:1, and Duolingo's text grey Eel #4B4B4B on white is 8.72:1 ([Canny](https://www.canny-creative.com/brand-breakdown/brand/duolingo-a-brand-breakdown/)). Expressiveness lives in the chroma of large fields, while meaning lives in lightness contrast that holds regardless of hue perception. Atlassian's "swap test" draws the line cleanly. If a colour could be swapped without changing meaning, it is free expressive territory, such as tags, illustration and surfaces. If not, it belongs to a semantic role ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color/accents/)). Practice converges on one dominant tinted neutral family, one signature hue for primary action and brand moments, and semantic hues only where they carry meaning. The "60-30-10" ratio has no traceable origin or test ([Apartment Therapy](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/interior-design-rule-60-30-10-explained-37504313)).

## Contrast and colourblind safety mostly align with beauty, except for charts

The strongest drivers of preference are hue similarity, lightness contrast, a light overall palette and gradient lightness. Those are the same properties that pass luminance contrast and survive deutan vision. Material's HCT makes the link exact: **a tone (L\*) difference of 40 guarantees WCAG ≥ 3:1, and 50 guarantees ≥ 4.5:1** ([M3 glossary](https://m3.material.io/foundations/glossary)). Radix's twelve-step scales guarantee APCA Lc 60 and Lc 90 for steps 11 and 12 on step 2 ([Radix](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale)). Colourblind observers' harmony judgments in food-colour studies "were not significantly different" from trichromats' ([IS&T](https://library.imaging.org/ei/articles/31/14/art00014)). Their single-colour preferences do shift: red–green dichromats like yellow most and blue much less ([Álvaro et al., PNAS 2015](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1502104112)). That evidence base is small, and every major harmony model excluded CVD observers.

There are three real conflicts. The first is **categorical colour**. Charts and status need hue separation, which harmony penalises, and red–green separation disappears for deutans. Colorgorical shows the trade-off can be managed. Raising perceptual distance and *name difference* weights cut chart-reading response times strongly (r = −.93 and −.89 for three-colour palettes), while raising pair-preference weight slowed responses. The resulting palettes were still as discriminable as ColorBrewer and Tableau and at least as preferred ([Gramazio, Laidlaw & Schloss 2017](https://vis.cs.brown.edu/docs/pdf/Gramazio-2016-CCD.pdf)). The axes deutans keep are lightness and blue–yellow (CIELAB b\*). Kuler raters' most positive feature was a bimodal blue/yellow spread in b\* ([O'Donovan et al. 2011](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~donovan/color/colorcomp.pdf)), so that separation is both liked and deutan-robust. Colour must still never be the only channel ([WCAG 1.4.1 via Baseline](https://baselinehq.com/blog/colourblindness-information-ui-design-red-green-problems-tips-tricks.html)). Redundant shape encoding helps most at 5–8 categories ([CatPAW](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06792)). The second conflict is **dark themes**. Harmony models reward high summed lightness, so a dark theme needs at least one high-lightness element and should avoid dark yellow and brown surfaces. WCAG 2 also overstates contrast near black, which is where APCA adds value ([Myndex](https://github.com/Myndex/SAPC-APCA/blob/master/documentation/WhyAPCA.md)). The third is **complementary accents**: they suit small figures and fail as large adjacent fields.

The available measurements sort into reliability tiers, and an API should report them that way.

| Tier | Signal | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hard constraint | WCAG 2.x ratio; APCA Lc; OKLab/CIEDE2000 ΔE between roles; ΔE under CVD simulation (Machado 2009 for protan/deutan with severity, Brettel 1997 for tritan) | Deterministic and validated; WCAG 2 is the legal floor ([DaltonLens](https://daltonlens.org/opensource-cvd-simulation/); [Machado](https://www.inf.ufrgs.br/~oliveira/pubs_files/CVD_Simulation/CVD_Simulation.html)) |
| Soft signal | Heer & Stone name difference; Colorgorical-style distance plus name scores | Validated against chart task speed ([Colorgorical](https://vis.cs.brown.edu/docs/pdf/Gramazio-2016-CCD.pdf)) |
| Advisory only | Ou harmony; O'Donovan LASSO score; lightness-gradient structure | Small or crowd samples, swatch stimuli, rewards typicality |

APCA's standards status is uncertain. One 2026 guide calls it WCAG 3's candidate method, but the research team's understanding is that W3C dropped it from drafts around 2023. The safe position is WCAG 2.x for compliance and APCA as an advisory check for dark mode and mid-tone fills.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "taste" means for an eyes-free agent. Harmony is a lightness-and-chroma structure with a few hue rules: few families, a gradient ramp, light yellows and dark blues, and hue contrast kept for small figures. A tool can enforce that structure, and it already overlaps with ChromaConscious's contrast and deutan constraints. What an agent adds is *meaning*: turning a brief into hue families and referents, which LLMs do at near-human accuracy for specific concepts. What it must not add is its prior, because the prior is the median, and median is the definition of the boring default. So the differentiator is not a better harmony score, which would pull toward typical. It is a loop in which the agent asks about intent, grounds hue choices in named, licensed, provenance-tagged references, lets the API solve and measure everything, and reports results as names and numbers a colourblind person can check.

Three uncertainties remain. No aesthetic model has been validated on rendered UI themes. No harmony model has been fitted on CVD observers. And no study tests whether scene, material or tradition descriptors actually improve LLM palettes. ChromaConscious could close the last two with its own users, and a deutan developer is well placed to recruit the panel that the literature has consistently screened out.

## Recommendations for ChromaConscious (not sourced findings)

These are the report author's recommendations. They build on the findings above and on the current `public/skill/SKILL.md`, but they are design proposals, not research results. They are listed in priority order.

### P0: return measurement the agent cannot do, in words it can relay

**1. Add a structured `quality` block to every generate and riff response, tiered as in the table above.** It should report per-role-pair WCAG ratio and APCA Lc (largely already present). It should also report min ΔE (OKLab) among chart series and among status colours, computed both normally and under Machado deutan and protan at severity 1.0 and Brettel tritan, and **name the colliding pair** ("chart-2 vs chart-4: ΔE 6 under deutan, both read 'brown'"). Add a lightness-structure check covering surface ramp monotonicity, ΔL\* between the text tier and the surface tier, and whether the theme includes a high-lightness element in dark mode. Add natural-order flags: any yellow-hue role (OKLCH h ≈ 90–110°) at CIELAB L\* below about 58, and any brown or olive surface. Include an Ou-style harmony number only if it is labelled `advisory`, and never let an agent optimise it.

**2. Describe every colour in agent-native and colourblind-checkable form.** Each swatch in a response should carry hex, OKLCH, a basic colour name and a lightness word ("dark navy, L 0.32"). Names can come from xkcd (CC0) or meodai/color-names (MIT), plus a Heer & Stone name-difference score (StanfordHCI c3, BSD-3 as reported downstream; read c3's own LICENSE before shipping). This follows ColorGPT's hex-plus-words finding and gives a deutan user names to verify against.

**3. Add a "sameness" distance.** Compute OKLCH distance from a reference set of default looks rather than a blacklist. The reference set: Tailwind indigo-500 or blue-500 (v4 blue-500 = oklch(62.3% 0.214 259.8)) on slate or zinc greys over white; purple-to-blue gradients; cream with terracotta; pure monochrome. Return it as `typicality` with the nearest default named. Banning one hue just moves the mode, so measure distance.

**4. Rewrite the SKILL.md agent guidance.** Add an explicit rule: "Do not estimate contrast, ΔE or colourblind safety yourself; quote the API." Add an anti-default section listing the default looks above, telling agents to pick hue from the brief or a reference, never from habit. Add 2–3 worked exemplars, each going from brief to parameters to resulting theme with its numbers. Add the deutan-safe expressive pattern: put boldness in the chroma of large surfaces and the signature hue, keep meaning in lightness steps, and pair status colours with icon or label.

### P1: elicit needs, then ground hue choices

**5. Ship a needs-elicitation script in SKILL.md, of at most six questions, each mapped to a parameter.** (a) Context and mode: light, dark or both, and long reading versus dashboards. This sets contrast level and APCA emphasis. (b) Accessibility, asked neutrally: "Does anyone in your audience have colour-vision differences?" This sets CVD ΔE floors. (c) Three Kobayashi-style bipolar choices: warm↔cool sets the hue range, soft↔hard sets lightness contrast and chroma, quiet↔vivid sets the chroma ceiling. (d) Three feeling or brand words, mapped by the agent to hue families. If the words are flat-association ("modern", "professional"), the agent should ask which concrete referent they evoke. (e) Fixed constraints: existing brand hex, colours to avoid, and the category's incumbent hues. (f) Conventional↔distinctive, mapped to typicality distance and riff hops. Then **present three deliberately different candidates** (quiet, warm/vivid, wildcard), each described in names, lightness and mood words with tool-verified facts. Iterate on verbal deltas ("warmer", "less loud", "more chart separation").

**6. Expose the brief axes as API parameters rather than leaving them in prose.** Example names: `warmth`, `vividness`, `hardness`, `distinct`, plus `avoid=hue-ranges` and `category=` for incumbent hues. Do not reuse `taste`, which already means fidelity to the input colours.

**7. Rebuild chart series around lightness and blue–yellow separation.** Your notes already record that the five chart colours collide in every preset and become identical under deutan. Score candidate series Colorgorical-style (ΔE plus name difference, with pair preference at a low weight). Enforce a deutan-simulated min-ΔE floor and a lightness spread. Recommend shape or label redundancy for five or more series in the export guidance.

**8. Add a grounded reference library (for example `/references`, searchable by mood, material and structure).** Each record should hold OKLCH anchors with role hints and area shares. It should hold structure as rules: lightness order, surface chroma ceiling, shared hue bias, depth rule. It should also hold referent words (positive-valence object names), CVD notes, a mandatory licence, and provenance. Re-apply the structure rules after the contrast solver moves lightness. That keeps a "blue-hour" or "iron-tannin" theme recognisable after accessibility fixes. The solver should move L, not hue. The licensing picture as found:

### P2: culture, taste diversity and validation

**9. Model cultural references as tiered, material-named records, and make none of them a default.** Tier A covers universal perceptual dimensions. Tier B covers supported deviations with citations (white with mourning in China, purple with mourning in Greece), surfaced as soft context flags. Tier C is folk claims, shown only as "verify with your audience." Name records by material and documented system ("JIS Z 8102 selection", "copper-green zellige glaze, pre-modern", "iron-tannin bògòlan tones", "lac red"), with endonym, period, process and an "approximate sRGB" label. Avoid ethnic labels. Ship colours only, never motifs. Do not name palettes after specific communities. Treat colours derived from Māori taonga as Māori data under Te Mana Raraunga ([principles](https://www.otago.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0014/321044/tmr-maori-data-sovereignty-principles-october-2018-832194.pdf)), and stay well clear of the "designs or elements" Mexico's 2022 cultural-heritage law protects ([Center for Art Law](https://itsartlaw.org/art-law/innovations-in-protecting-art-and-design-placing-mexicos-recent-ban-on-cultural-appropriation-in-greater-context/)). Prefer community-contributed or community-reviewed records. Let audience shift *colourfulness* range, not hue.

**10. Expose a harmony↔striking control and treat taste as clustered.** Users agree only 52% of the time, so offer a single control (or a few named taste clusters) instead of claiming one optimum, and learn from which of the three candidates users pick.

**11. Run the study the literature skipped.** Have CVD and trichromat participants rate rendered UI themes. Compare default versus grounded generation, and Ou harmony computed on simulated versus original colours. This would validate both the advisory score and the reference library, and it would be publishable evidence that the agent-first approach produces better themes.
