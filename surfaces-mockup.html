what each lever actually moves

Both levers touch only the 15 tokens that descend from neutral. Nothing else in the theme changes — primary, accent and the status colours are untouched, which is why the frame stays recognisably the same theme across all nine cells.

separationthe lightness spacing of background, card, popover, secondary, muted, sidebar, border, input.
tintthe chroma those same steps keep from your neutral's hue — plus, at strong, a real primary mix into secondary.

the thing the code told us

ChromaConscious's current rule is one step inward from the page extreme. Measured on a real palette:

light — page #f6fff3, card #f2fbef · card is darker
dark — page #0e140c, card #131911 · card is lighter

Coherent in ramp-space, but perceptually it means cards are recessed in light and elevated in dark. That asymmetry is why the three settings can't be one formula:

  • flat — every surface at the ramp extreme; borders do all the work. This is what shadcn ships by default (card and background are both pure white).
  • layered — today's behaviour, one step inward.
  • lifted — the page steps back so card can reach the extreme and popover can go past it. In light mode that's a grey page with white cards; in dark mode pure lightness is enough.

review: does light actually read as light?

Fair challenge — measured against generateTheme, the first pass of this sheet was wrong twice:

enginelight page L .976 / .941 · dark page L .006
wasflat 1.000 (pure white — killed the tint lever, no gamut room for chroma) · lifted .886 (visibly grey)
nowtop step pinned at 98.5, light stack held inside ~93–98.5

Root cause: the plans were hardcoded absolutes instead of being anchored to the range the engine emits. ChromaConscious never emits pure white — and it shouldn't, because a tinted neutral needs headroom to carry chroma.

the honest problem with lifted

One part is not a bug and can't be fixed with numbers: to raise card above the page in light mode, the page has to come down. Colour-only elevation and a near-white page are mutually exclusive. lifted now dips the page just ~2 points and lets a shadow carry the rest — which is exactly the shadow-token gap, showing up as "light mode looks grey".

trySet lifted + light and look at the dropdown: it's separated by shadow, not colour. Now switch to dark — the same setting separates it by lightness alone. That's the asymmetry, and it's the one thing here that isn't purely a colour decision.

Three ways out, in increasing cost: cap "lifted" in light so it does nothing there (honest, but a dead lever); emit a small shadow scale alongside the colours; or grey the page enough for colour alone to work (what the first pass did — and what you caught). Only the middle one keeps light mode white AND gives real elevation, so the shadow scale is looking less optional than I framed it two sheets ago.

on tint

The frame makes the ceiling obvious: strong reads as branded on the sidebar and the secondary button, but the large card fields are where it turns muddy first. The cap matters more than the curve.

tryCompare none and subtle at layered. It's a small move, and it's roughly where ChromaConscious already sits — the neutral's own hue leaks in through the ramp whether or not we expose a control. So none is a genuinely new capability: it's the only way to get true greys out of a tinted neutral.

Building this caught a trap worth writing down. My first pass drove tint with HSL saturation and it was invisible on light surfaces — because chroma in HSL is s · (1 − |2L − 1|), so at L≈99% even 100% saturation yields nothing. Tint has to be a target chroma, solved back into whatever space you're in. The engine's OKLCH ramp gets this for free; anything reimplementing the preview in HSL will hit the same wall.

dimensionality — is it in scope?

Checked the engine before answering: it emits colour only. themeTokensJson hardcodes $type:'color', and there is no radius or shadow anywhere in src/engine/. But shadcn's own contract ships --radius, and tweakcn edits colour, radius, shadow and typography. So "colour generator" is already a narrower claim than the format expects.

The stronger argument is that a shadow is a colour decision:

  • Every shadow here is tinted by the neutral's hue — light mode uses rgba(33,58,59,…), not black, because a pure-black shadow over a warm or cool theme reads dirty. That tint comes free from the ramp ChromaConscious already builds; it needs no new user input.
  • The alphas are mode-derived, and they have to be. Dark has almost no absolute luminance room — page L .006 to card L .013 — so dark shadows run 5–8× the light alphas just to register.
  • Dark also gets a top inset highlight that shadow can't provide: inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,.075). That single line does more for perceived elevation on dark than any shadow.
Which reframes the whole control: separation is the border↔shadow balance, not just lightness spacing. flat pays for separation with hairlines and no shadow; lifted softens the hairline to 44% and lets shadow carry it. The border colour mixes toward the surface as the shadow grows.

trySet overlay: dialog and walk flat → layered → lifted in each mode. Four elevation levels, one scrim, all derived from one neutral. Then look at the ladder: flat in light gives level 1 no shadow at all — correct, that's shadcn's default look.

Cost to ship: four tokens (--elevation-1/2/3, --scrim), one non-colour $type in the DTCG export, and no new UI — they fall out of separation, which already exists.

the frame was lying — fixed

You caught a real one. With the dialog open, the token readout said background #f3f9f9 while the frame rendered rgb(176,188,188) — I measured the actual pixel. The values were right; the scrim was dimming the entire frame by ~27%, so every surface you were trying to compare was being tinted by an overlay.

A reference frame has one job: be truthful about the colours it declares. Putting a scrim over it defeats the whole sheet. The modal now lives in its own specimen below the ladder, where dimming is the point rather than a lie — and the frame is never dimmed.

I also split level 1's two shadow layers apart. They were 0 1px 2px and 0 1px 3px — near-identical, so the contact shadow and the key shadow collapsed into one smudge. Now 0 2px 6px -1px for the key, which is what makes a card read as lifted rather than just outlined.
